DECISIONGRAPH — GOVERNED DECISIONS IN MOTION
Target duration: approximately 2 minutes

SLIDE 1 — OPENING
AI agents can make recommendations. DecisionGraph makes those recommendations
governable. In the next two minutes, we’ll follow one inventory decision from
trusted DataHub context, through human approval, into the catalog—and then
safely revalidate it when conditions change.

SLIDE 2 — THE PROBLEM
A useful recommendation is not enough. A governed organization must also prove
which data shaped it, who approved it, what action followed, and how the
decision responds when the underlying evidence changes. DecisionGraph captures
that missing operational memory.

SLIDE 3 — CONTROLLED LIFECYCLE
The workflow begins when the agent runs. It stores a pending decision instead
of taking action immediately. A human approval unlocks the DataHub projection.
If conditions later change, invalidation marks the decision for review, and
revalidation creates a fresh replacement with a traceable supersedes
relationship.

SLIDE 4 — GOVERNED MCP CONTEXT
At decision time, the agent calls the official DataHub MCP server. It retrieves
the governed entities and their schema fields. DecisionGraph stores the context
source, exact tool names, fetch time, selected facts, and a pruned snapshot
beside the recommendation—so the evidence is inspectable later.

SLIDE 5 — EVIDENCE BEFORE APPROVAL
The prototype’s sample decision identifies three governed Northeast reorder
candidates, confirms fresh forecast coverage within the configured horizon, and
records the five inventory plus five forecast fields considered. That proof is
visible before the approver can authorize any catalog write.

SLIDE 6 — DATAHUB PROJECTION
After approval, DecisionGraph projects a real DataHub Document. The document
receives its own URN and links back to both governed datasets. The live test
read the artifact back successfully, confirming two related datasets and a
non-global scope.

SLIDE 7 — SAFE REVALIDATION
When a source condition changes, DecisionGraph stores the invalidation event
even if no current decision matches. Revalidation then fetches fresh MCP
context before creating a replacement. The original remains intact, and the
new decision points back through a unique supersedes edge.

SLIDE 8 — CLOSE
The result is a closed loop for accountable AI decisions: governed context
enters, a reviewable recommendation is created, human approval controls
projection, and later changes trigger traceable revalidation. The prototype
passed all twelve automated checks, live MCP retrieval, real DataHub projection
readback, browser workflow, and mobile layout verification.

DecisionGraph: run, review, trace, and revalidate.
